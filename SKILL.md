# Writing Craft: First Thoughts

Treat generation and editing as different jobs. In this pass, get live material onto the page; do not ask the generation pass to make that material finished.

## Core rules

### 1. Preserve first thoughts

Write the initial, unedited perception rather than the safer version the writer thinks is expected. Prefer the concrete, strange, uncomfortable, or unusually phrased thought when it carries genuine energy. Do not soften it merely because it is less polite or less conventional.

### 2. Keep moving

Move forward without rereading, correcting, optimizing, or line-editing. If a sentence is awkward, leave it and continue. Do not perfect the opening at the expense of reaching the idea that may appear later.

### 3. Go toward the charge

When the prose wants to back off into a generality, move closer to the specific moment, image, conflict, desire, or admission. Replace “the meeting was difficult” with what happened in the room, but do not invent drama that is not supported by the user's material.

### 4. Overproduce

Generate surplus and range: several openings, alternate images, different angles, the full tangent, or deliberately unlikely possibilities. Generation optimizes for material and choice, not quality. Most of the output may be cut later; that is the point.

### 5. Start with a constraint

When the page is blank or the user is stalled, impose a small container: a timer, one prompt, a forced first word, a fixed length, one scene, or one question. Narrow choice to restore motion. For example: “Write for five minutes about the moment you knew it went wrong.”

## Generation workflow

1. Identify the material to generate and the intended scale: one line, several openings, a paragraph, a scene, an argument, or a freewrite.
2. Choose a constraint if the request is open-ended or stalled.
3. Generate forward in a concrete, energetic, and varied mode. Keep multiple possibilities distinct rather than blending them into a safe average.
4. Do not silently revise while generating. If useful, mark uncertainty with a placeholder and continue.
5. Label the result as raw material, options, or a first pass when that distinction matters.
6. Offer the next pass only after the material exists: structure, point, flow, intensification, audience, or cutting. Do not perform it automatically unless requested.

## Boundaries and failure modes

- Raw is not finished. Preserve the energy now, but expect later shaping.
- Abundance is input to subtraction, not permission to keep everything.
- Energy is not an alibi for unsupported claims, clichés, or arbitrary sloppiness.
- Do not confuse generation with editing. If the user asks for a polished final version, use an appropriate revision skill or explicitly run a separate revision pass after generating material.
- Preserve the user's facts, intent, voice, and boundaries. Take risks in phrasing and specificity, not with invented evidence.

## Output behavior

When applying the skill to a user's writing task, end with a brief craft note naming the two or three generation moves that did the most work and why. Keep it high level; do not narrate every sentence or produce a technique audit. If the user supplied multiple craft skills, give one combined note for the whole response.
